Starting Your Experience

Begin Your Partnership with Confidence

You have reflected on your goals and researched your context - now it is time to turn preparation into action. This phase is about formally launching the relationship with your partner, agreeing on how your team will work together, and setting the project in motion with a shared plan everyone understands.

Why the First Weeks Set the Tone

How a team and partner begin working together shapes everything that follows. Teams that take time to build trust, agree on expectations, and clarify roles up front tend to move faster and communicate more openly once real project work begins.

Skipping this step, or assuming everyone already agrees on how the project will run, is one of the most common causes of confusion and conflict later on. A short investment in explicit agreements during the starting phase pays off across the entire experience.

Phase 2: Starting the Experience

Use this phase to establish relationships, define roles and responsibilities, set expectations, build a working project plan, and agree on how your team and partner will communicate.

Meet Your Partner and Build the Relationship

Your first conversations with a partner do more than exchange information - they establish trust. Come prepared with thoughtful questions, listen for what matters most to your partner, and be transparent about your team's capacity and timeline.

Treat this as the start of a working relationship, not a single kickoff meeting. Early, consistent communication signals that your team is reliable and invested in a mutually beneficial outcome.

Starting Goals

  • Establish relationships: Introduce your team, learn about your partner's organization, and begin building mutual trust and rapport.
  • Define roles and responsibilities: Clarify who owns which parts of the project, both within your team and between your team and the partner.
  • Set expectations: Agree on deliverables, timelines, communication frequency, and what success looks like for everyone involved.
  • Develop team and project plans: Turn goals into a concrete plan with milestones, deadlines, and assigned owners.
  • Establish communication practices: Decide how and how often your team and partner will check in, share updates, and raise concerns.

Questions to Consider

  • What does my partner need from this project, and how do they define success?
  • Who on my team is responsible for which tasks, and how will we hold each other accountable?
  • How often should we communicate with our partner, and through what channels?
  • What happens if the scope, timeline, or team availability changes?
  • What written agreement or plan will we refer back to if questions come up later?

Recommended Starting Process

  1. Hold a kickoff conversation: Meet with your partner to introduce your team, confirm goals, and ask clarifying questions.
  2. Draft a team charter: Document roles, responsibilities, working norms, and how your team will make decisions together.
  3. Build a project plan: Break the work into milestones with realistic deadlines and identify who owns each piece.
  4. Agree on communication norms: Set expectations for meeting cadence, response times, and how updates will be shared with your partner.
  5. Confirm alignment: Share your plan with your partner and adjust based on their feedback before moving into active work.

What's Next

Once your team and partner have a shared plan in place, you are ready to move into active project work.
